OPENING THE ROV IN FUSION 360 (or Onshape, FreeCAD, SolidWorks, any CAD app that reads STEP)

TinkerSub_KB-1_assembly.step
    The whole ROV in one file: every part is a named, coloured component in its assembled position, grouped by
    subsystem (Thrusters, Frame and side plates, Top cowl, Hull, Electronics sled, Battery pod, Lights).
    Units mm, Z up, front of the ROV toward -Y.
    Fusion: Data Panel > Upload > pick the file (or File > Open > Open from my computer). It opens as a design with
    one component per part. If it lies on its back, your Fusion is set to Y up:
    Preferences > General > Design > Default modeling orientation = Z up, then re-open.

    Full detail (edit these directly): stations, corner brackets, junction deck, belly pan, cowl sections,
    HDPE side plates, battery holder plates, junction boxes, pod caps, sled rear bulkhead.
    Reference only (simplified to keep the file small): thrusters, props, flanges, dome, lights, pipes, cells,
    electronics tray and camera parts. Use their full-detail files below when you want to edit them.

parts/<name>.step
    Every printed part on its own at full detail, in print orientation (the same as the STL folder), with its
    debossed labels, named like the STLs. Edit one, export STL, print. Hold Shift and select several in the Upload dialog to bring them all in.

TinkerSub_KB-1_assembly_mesh.3mf
    The whole ROV as full-resolution meshes, in position. Fusion: Insert > Insert Mesh. Light and fast for checking
    fit around a part you are editing.

Parts with no STEP file
    101 housing, 102 tail cone (fine threads), 103 / 104 props and 105 nose cone (curved blades and cones), and the
    hand-drawn parts 201, 203 and 207 (hull flanges, sled front bulkhead): converted from mesh to STEP they would be
    50-250 MB each, so their STLs are the reference. To edit one in Fusion: Insert > Insert Mesh with the STL, then
    Mesh > Modify > Convert Mesh, method "Prismatic" (paid Fusion plans; turns flats, cylinders and holes into real faces).
    212-215 (sled), 305 and 306 (battery) are true CAD solids, drawn in the sled / plate frame rather than print orientation.

Part numbers
    Every file starts with the part's three-digit number (601_rov_station_top_port.step), the same number that is
    debossed on the printed part and used in the assembly guide. 901 / 902 are the HDPE side plates.

How editable are they?
    These parts were generated by Python scripts, not drawn in Fusion, so there is no sketch or timeline history.
    In Fusion they are solid bodies: flat faces, pockets, slots and the nickel-strip grooves come in as single clean
    faces you can Press Pull, Move, Offset, cut and join. Curved surfaces and round holes come in as narrow flat
    facets; to resize a round hole, select its facets and use Press Pull, or cut a new hole with a cylinder.
    The fully parametric source is the Python in Source/ (one number changes every part that uses it).
